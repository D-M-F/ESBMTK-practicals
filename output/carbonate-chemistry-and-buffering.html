Large buffer capacity means a small pH response to a given small TA change. Sensitivity is the reciprocal of the total capacity; reciprocals of the separate contributions do not add. These are local derivatives, not finite pH changes or the pCO2–DIC sensitivity shown above.

Definitions, assumptions and source

Inspired by Middelburg (2019), Marine Carbon Biogeochemistry, Figs 5.3 and 5.8 (printed pp. 88 and 98), and Eqs 5.18, 5.20 and 5.21. Original calculations are reproduced conceptually, not digitized. The book's Fig. 5.3 uses 15 °C and DIC = 2000 µM; this explorer uses the displayed course conditions (16 °C) and mass-specific concentrations. Peak positions and heights therefore need not match the book exactly.

PyCO2SYS calculates equilibria on the seawater pH scale (scale 2). B is evaluated by centred differences in pH (±0.0001), holding DIC, T/S/P and other analytical totals fixed. Carbonate, borate and water contributions are the corresponding derivatives of [HCO3⁻] + 2[CO3²⁻], borate alkalinity and [OH⁻] − [H⁺]free. The remainder includes sulfate/fluoride acid–base terms; keeping it closes the full TA derivative over the broad pH range. Nutrient totals retain the course defaults.

B is shown in µeq kg⁻¹ per pH unit, while S is shown in pH units per (eq kg⁻¹). Thus the plotted numerical values satisfy B × S = 10⁶. The seawater approximation is B ≈ ln(10) K₂ DIC/[H⁺], with K₂ and H⁺ on the same pH scale; it is displayed only between pH 7.5 and 8.5. The 0–14 view illustrates acid–base mathematics at fixed salinity and analytical totals, not a prediction of real seawater at extreme pH.

Moving the DIC slider moves the vertical cut and updates every diagram. Moving TA moves state A along that cut; state B is a nearby point on the same cut. The TA–pH diagram replots the exact states sampled on the contour grid. Its tangent is the local derivative; the A–B slope is a finite-interval comparison. Shading on the extended buffer diagrams identifies the pH interval visible on the upper contour map.
