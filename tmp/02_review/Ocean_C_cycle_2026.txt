Solubility product is not constant
• Solubility product in seawater: Ksp= ([Ca2+]sat[CO32-]sat)
• Solubility product depends on environmental factors such as:
–  Salinity (more soluble in seawater than freshwater)
– Temperature (colder more soluble, higher Ksp)
– Pressure (higher more soluble, higher Ksp)
– Mineral form (Calcite vs. Aragonite)

Saturation Horizon and CCD
Saturation horizon: set by mineral and carbonate chemistry (S,T, P, ..) 
Carbonate compensation depth (CCD) : depth where downward flux of carbonate 
particles is balanced by dissolution rate
Lysocline: zone between saturation horizon and CCD
Snow line: depth where no carbonate is found in sediment

Anthropogenic carbon in the ocean
• Carbon in the ocean participates in multiple processes (biological, chemical, organic/inorganic).
• Anthropogenic carbon is not a directly measurable quantity. It can only be estimated 
indirectly and is due to physical-chemistry and increasing atmospheric carbon dioxide.
• The anthropogenic signal in the ocean is only a few percent of the (unknown) natural 
background of dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC).

Anthropogenic carbon in the ocean
• 90% of DIC in ocean is chemical equilibrium ocean/atmosphere
• 10% of DIC is due to biological pump
• Anthropogenic DIC (Cant) is very small addition due to increase in 
atmospheric carbon dioxide.
• Important to distinguish between (1) DIC natural, (2) climate change 
induced DIC changes and (3) anthropogenic DIC.

PAGE 64
Carbonate Compensation Dynamics
• At steady-state: 
– CCD equals snow-line
– Saturation depth is 1 km shallower than CCD
• During Acidification:
– Saturation depth and CCD diverge and shallow rapidly
– Snowline rises slowly is uncoupled from CCD
• During transition from high to low CO2 world:
– Saturation depth and CCD converge but remain different
– CCD approaches snowline
