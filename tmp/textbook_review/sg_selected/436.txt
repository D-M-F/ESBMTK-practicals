The Atmospheric Oxygen Method
The anthropogenic CO
2 inventory method permitted
us to develop an anthropogenic CO 2 budget for the
Anthropocene, but we are equally interested in con-
straining the budget for the last few decades. In the
absence of direct measurements of the global oceanic
increase in DIC, a very elegant method to constrain the
oceanic uptake of anthropogenic CO
2 since the early
1990s is based on simultaneous measurements of at-
mospheric oxygen and CO
2 pioneered by Keeling and
Shertz [1992].
The method builds upon the fact that O2 and CO2 are
tightly coupled during the formation and oxidation of
organic matter (including the burning of fossil fuels), but
that these two gases are exchanged independently be-
tween the ocean and atmosphere. If one then assumes
that the long-term exchange of O
2 with the ocean is zero
or is small and can be estimated from independent
sources, one ends up with two balance equations and two
unknowns (the exchange of CO
2 with the ocean and land
biosphere, respectively), which can be solved.
